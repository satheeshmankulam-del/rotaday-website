Set the pattern once, tap which day of the cycle you are on today, and every 4 on 4 off shift for the next year appears on your calendar.

What 4 on 4 off means

Four days on, four days off, repeating on an eight-day cycle. Because eight does not divide into seven, your working days drift across the week — this week you work the weekend, in four weeks you do not. That is exactly why it is painful to track in a normal calendar, and why typing it in by hand stops being worth it after a month or two.

Many employers run it as four day shifts, four off, four night shifts, four off — a sixteen-day cycle. RotaWake handles both, and any other cycle length you need.

How RotaWake handles it

Built in already

4 on 4 off is one of the preset patterns. Pick it from the list — there is no setup.

One tap to anchor it

Tap which day of the cycle you are on today and the whole rotation lines up, backwards and forwards.

Days and nights

Running the sixteen-day days-and-nights version? That is a preset too.

Swaps don’t break it

Cover someone else’s shift and the underlying rotation carries on untouched.

Why not a spreadsheet

A rota in a spreadsheet is a list of dates somebody typed. It is right until the first swap, the first leave day, or the first month you forget to extend it. RotaWake stores the rule instead of the dates, so it never runs out and never drifts.
